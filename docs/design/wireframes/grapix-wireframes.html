GrapiX Editor, built around the broadcast job

Not After Effects, and not EffectCraft's panels. A broadcast graphic has a short life: someone makes the style, gives it a few moments of motion, connects it to live data, proves it works with real names and scores, and sends it to an operator who takes it on air. These wireframes organise the Editor around that. The Editor is the design studio: nothing in it is live. Today it has two readers: Playout, and AI models over MCP (Claude, ChatGPT and others). A planned module would also feed Prism, which makes automatic highlights, with overlays designed in Figma, animated and data-bound here. Live on air is Playout, not the Editor: its centre is the sequence of takes, redrawn below. Low fidelity: placement and workflow, not final styling.

The idea: one graphic, five steps

The unit is a graphic (what operators call a template): a lower third, a scorebug, a full-screen. Everything on screen answers one of five questions. The words are the ones a producer or operator already uses.

StepQuestionWord we useInstead of
1 StyleWhat does it look like?Parts (shapes, text, images) and Effects (glow, shadow, wipe edge)Layers, effects, layer styles
2 MotionHow does it behave on air?Moments: Appears, Stays, Changes, Leaves, each with a chosen styleA keyframe timeline (still there under "Fine-tune")
3 DataWhat changes per use?Fields, set on the part itself: fixed, or from a fieldA separate bindings table
4 TestDoes it survive real content?Test records: sample records stepped through the graphic, with problems flaggedPreview by hand, find out on air
5 PublishWhere does it go, and is it ready for each place?Formats (16:9, 9:16, 1:1) and Destinations (Playout, other SKWAD apps, files, link), with a checklist per destinationA single publish button for one engine
  1. One screen, always the same three things: the parts on the left, the stage in the middle, and a single card for the selected part showing its Style, Motion and Data stacked. A designer works on all three at once, because that is the job; there are no workspaces to switch.
  2. Below the stage, a Moments strip (what happens when it appears, stays, changes and leaves) The test records are not a strip here: they live in the Data step (B4), where every record is checked at once.
  3. The graphic is not tied to one screen shape. It has formats that share the same parts, and it publishes a package: one numbered, unchangeable version that any destination can read. Playout takes the 16:9 format today; formats for Prism and social posts are planned.
  4. A journey rail at the top shows the five steps as done, needs attention or blocked. "Fix next issue" jumps to the first problem. The operator's form is built from the same fields, so there is nothing to keep in sync.
  5. Effects and presets become Effects: a short gallery of house effects first. The full engine catalogue is one click behind "All effects"; the engine says which ones it can render.
  6. Everything else is found with search (Ctrl K): commands, parts, effects, fields and help in one box. That keeps the menus short.

Workflows: now and planned

The Editor is the design studio and is never live. Today it has two readers of its designs: Playout (live on air) and AI models through MCP (Claude, ChatGPT and any other model that speaks MCP). Everything else below is planned, so the design must not block it, but nothing here is to be built yet.

WorkflowStatusWhoPath
1 Live on-air graphicnowDesigner, then Playout operatorDesign in the Editor (from a house graphic or a Figma frame), publish, take from the Playout sequence by Take ID
2 AI-assisted design and controlnowDesigner or operator with any AI modelThe model connects over MCP: finds graphics, reads their fields, previews, proposes edits for the designer to accept, and can ask to publish. It can never take, preload or clear Program.
3 Highlight overlays for PrismplannedPrism, a designer, an AI modelA request arrives, a Figma frame is made, GrapiX animates it and binds the data, the finished file goes to Prism, which packages it for the end customer. See the next section.
4 Social posts and vertical overlaysplannedSocial designerSame route as 3: Figma frame in 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5, animated and data-bound in GrapiX, delivered as a file or package.

What keeps this open for later

  1. One package. A publish produces an unchangeable, numbered package: parts, moments, the list of fields (name, type, rules), formats, assets and a fingerprint. Playout reads it today; Prism or any other reader could read it later without changing the design. Editing afterwards makes a new number (invariant 7).
  2. MCP is the door for AI models. Tools such as graphics.list, graphics.get, graphics.fields and graphics.render_preview read; graphics.propose_edit needs the designer to accept; graphics.publish_request needs approval. Program controls are not offered at all (invariants 4 and 5).
  3. Destinations are a short list with stated abilities. Playout today; Prism later. Each says which formats it takes and how it receives, and Publish checks the package against it.
  4. Figma stays linked. A Figma frame comes in as parts; a changed frame can be pulled again and only the differences are shown to accept.

Planned module (idea only, not to be built yet): overlays for Prism

Prism makes highlights automatically. Each highlight needs an overlay design, which a designer currently makes by hand. The idea is to produce them from a design once and fill them with data automatically.

StepWhereWhat happens
1 RequirementPrism, or a person"We need a goal overlay for esports match highlights" (kind of highlight, sport or game, format, event style).
2 DesignFigmaA designer, or an AI model through MCP, makes the Figma frame once per overlay type and format (16:9, 9:16, 1:1).
3 Animate and bindGrapiX EditorThe frame comes in as parts, gets its moments (appears, stays, leaves) and its fields (player, team, score, time), and is checked against real sample records.
4 PackageGrapiXAn animated, data-linked package is published: the style, the motion, the field list and a version number.
5 Fill automaticallyPrismFor each highlight Prism supplies the data and gets the finished overlay; it packages the highlight for the end customer and shares it.
6 Social postsSame routeSquare, vertical or 4:5 posts use the same designs and data.
  1. Data binding, effects and motion are all done in GrapiX; Figma holds only the design.
  2. The Editor would gain one screen, Requests (wireframe F1 below): what Prism asked for, which have a design, which need one, and which are published.
  3. What Prism needs depends on questions only you can answer (see decisions 6 to 8): who produces the final video or image, and in what file format.

Playout in plain words

Live on air happens only in Playout. Its heart is the sequence: the ordered list of things that will go on air during a show.

Plain wordWhat it isWhy it matters
TakeOne entry in the sequence: a scene, ready to go on airHas its own Take ID, a number that never changes when the list is reordered
Take IDThe address of a take, for example 1003. Exists only in Playout; the operator types it by handAny system (TCP, UDP, HTTP, a custom protocol) can call "take 1003". Must be unique in the show. Names can change; the ID keeps its meaning.
Scene IDThe ID of the scene itself, for example s-021. Set in the scene list and shown the same in the Editor and in PlayoutThe same scene can be used by several takes; its ID does not change when it is renamed
LayerA stacking slot on the output, a whole number from -99,999 to +99,999. 0 is the default; plus is toward the front, minus toward the backBackgrounds and foregrounds play together, each on its own layer, and each layer is cued, cued out and cleared on its own
Scene listAll scenes available to play out, with their versionsScenes are dragged from here into the sequence; name and description are editable
Scene dataThe fields a scene lets the operator changeOnly fields the designer published appear; values are typed by hand or linked to a data source
PreviewA small player in the Playout layout (360 or 480 lines, lowers itself under load): play, pause, stop, loopTotally separate from what is on air, on its own renderer, so nothing there can change Program
On air (Program)What the viewers seeOnly Take, Continue, Hold, Take out and Clear act on it
Virtual outputA pop-up window at the full output resolution (1080p or 4K) showing what you send to itCheck the true look of a take locally. Independent of the live output; costs GPU

Rule that ties it together: "TAKE" sends the exact version and data that were checked in Preview or on the virtual output, and the engine refuses if they no longer match. What the operator watched is what goes out.

Decisions for you

1 Vocabulary

These are working names only. Several echo other products, so every name is changeable in one place (see "Names" above): rename a term there and every screen on this page updates.

2 Keyframe timeline (decided)

The timeline is keyframe based and is the only motion screen. There are no template animations and no phase bands. See B3b in the Figma screens below.

3 One selected-part card

Style, Motion and Data stacked on one card, replacing separate Properties, Effect Controls, Bindings and Animation panels. Too dense, or just right?

4 No preset layouts (decided)

The Editor does not predefine scene layouts. The first screen offers a blank graphic or an import from Figma, PSD or SVG.

5 Six menus plus Ctrl K

File, Edit, Graphic, Publish, View, Help, with search for everything else.

6 Prism: who makes the final file

Does GrapiX render the finished overlay (video with alpha, or image), or does Prism play GrapiX's animated, data-linked package itself? This decides the file format and whether GrapiX needs a render service.

7 Prism: where the data comes from

For each highlight, what does Prism know (player, team, score, time, game, clip length)? That list becomes the fields every overlay design uses.

8 Which AI models first, and how much they may do

Claude and ChatGPT are named. Should every model get the same limits (read, propose, ask to publish), or different ones per model?

9 Scene ID rules

Settled: Scene ID is needed in the Editor and Playout; Take ID only in Playout, typed by the operator. Still to decide: is the Scene ID made automatically when a scene is created, can it be edited later, and what happens to takes if it changes?

13 Transition setting

Settled: layers -99,999 to +99,999, default 0, higher in front, a take on an occupied layer replaces it (out then in, overlap or cut, with an offset). Still to decide: is the transition set per layer, per take, or both?

14 Scene logic first version

JavaScript only first, or JavaScript plus the Nodes view from the start? I suggest JavaScript first, Nodes right after, on one shared set of actions and events.

10 Which fields can Playout change

The designer publishes fields in the Editor. In Playout I let the operator type values, link them, and hide ones they do not need. Should the operator also be able to publish extra fields, or only the designer?

11 Linked values on air

Safe default: a linked value changes on air only when the operator presses Update, unless auto-update is allowed per field. Agree?

12 Commands and vendor protocols

Which control systems must call takes first, and over which protocol? The command words in P4 are examples only.
