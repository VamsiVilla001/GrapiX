# 2.24 Asset plane: authentication, resume, retry, transport errors

**Slice 1 (authentication) built 19 November 2026; slices 2 to 4 (resume and retry, duplicates, transport errors) built the same day. The row is done.** User decision (asked, 19 Nov 2026): the asset plane **reuses the control plane's gx1 token** (no separate asset credential).

The row has four parts (the credential; resume from the last whole chunk with retry and backoff; an idempotent concurrent duplicate upload; a reset or end of stream reported as a transport error), from findings 3, 7, 8 and 14 of the OSI audit. Slice 1 is the credential (finding 3); slices 2 to 4 follow below (findings 7 and 14).

## Slice 1: what was built
- **Policy:** `Plane::Asset` now authenticates (`Shared/contracts/src/endpoints.rs`): an asset listener with a secret may leave loopback (the secret must meet the policy's 32-byte minimum), one without may not.
- **`gx_asset_plane::auth`** (new): the credential rides a request's first frame as an `auth` member, as the correlation id does (2.13), read and removed by the listener before it checks the request has exactly one member; a transfer's later frames carry none (verified once per connection). A client works under a credential by `auth::enter(credential)` (a scope, like a correlation id, so no public client function gained an argument) or `auth::enter_from_environment()` (`GX_ASSET_CREDENTIAL`).
- **`AssetPublishServer::bind_authenticating(addr, peer, secret)`:** every request but the locality read needs the **Author** scope; the locality read needs any credential that verifies. A request with none, or one that does not verify, is refused `Unauthenticated`; one that verifies without Author is refused `ScopeNotGranted`, each **in the reply type its request expects** (`PublishRefusal::AuthRefused { cause }` for publish, package and locality; `Refused { cause }` for fetch, preview and transfer), before the peer is reached. `asset.authenticated` and `asset.refused` are on the log with the subject and the fault's word, never the credential.
- **The engine:** with `--token` the asset plane verifies the same credential and follows `--bind` (it stayed on loopback "until it verifies a credential of its own"); without a token it is unchanged.
- **Clients:** the Editor's publish and preview, the mock editor and the Playout MCP's image fetch work under `GX_ASSET_CREDENTIAL`; the Editor shell tells a refused publisher what to set.
- **`gx-credential mint`** (new command): `gx-credential mint --subject <who> --role <editor|playout|engine> [--scope <name>]... [--secret-env <NAME> | --secret <value>]` prints a credential made with the engine's secret (default variable `GX_TOKEN_SECRET`; a secret shorter than the policy minimum is refused, never repeated back). Until now only the mock Playout could make one, so a protected engine could not be used by a person.

## Evidence (slice 1)
- `gx-asset-plane`: 130 lib tests, `tests/auth.rs` 6 (every client function refused by name with no credential and the peer never reached; a credential made with another secret, an altered one and a non-gx1 one refused as `Unauthenticated` and never echoed; an Author credential makes every request and a transfer's later frames need none; a credential without Author reads locality and nothing else; an unprotected listener ignores a credential; only an authenticating listener leaves loopback and a weak secret is refused) and 4 unit tests in `auth.rs`; `gx-credential` 12 (the `mint` command); `gx-contracts` endpoints 8.
- **The real engine binary** (`gx-render-worker --bind 0.0.0.0 --token <secret>`, `tests/bind.rs`): the asset plane is off loopback; no credential, another secret's, a Playout credential's publish (no Author) are each refused by name; an Author credential is answered. On this machine's own network address both planes answer and neither answers on loopback.
- Conformance 632 passed, 0 failed, 9 skipped.
- 14 mutants, one at a time: 13 each failed a named test (credential not verified, Author not required, locality needing Author, credential never sent, not sent on a transfer's first frame, not removed from the request, empty secret, bind policy not asked, anything writable, guard not restoring, the policy classification, the mint command's minimum and both-secrets rules, and the engine ignoring the token for the asset plane). One survived and is equivalent: writing the locality refusal as a `PublishReply` instead of a `LocalityReply` produces the identical JSON (both are `{"reply":"refused", ...AuthRefused}`), so no test can tell them apart.

## Slices 2 to 4: resume, retry, duplicates, transport errors
- **A transfer belongs to its content hash, not its connection.** The receiver keeps unfinished transfers in one table shared by every connection (`Partials`, at most 16, the oldest dropped past that; each is already bounded by 2.22's chunk limit). `Begin` of an asset it holds part of answers `Started { resume_from }` with the number of whole verified chunks, where it used to answer 0; `Begin` of one it holds whole answers `resume_from = total_chunks` (nothing left to send), where it used to refuse `AssetTransferAlreadyComplete`; a `Begin` with a different length under the same name starts over; a transfer that fails verification leaves nothing to resume (invariant 28 is unchanged: bytes are promoted only after the whole is verified).
- **A duplicate is answered, not refused.** A chunk already delivered (a retry whose reply was lost, or the other of two uploaders got there first) is acknowledged and counted once if its bytes are the same and a whole chunk long, and refused by name (`AssetChunkOutOfOrder`) if they differ. The last chunk of an asset another connection has just finished is answered `Stored`. The table stays locked through verification and promotion, so there is no moment when a transfer is finished and not yet held.
- **The client resumes and retries.** `AssetTransferClient::with_retry(RetryPolicy)` (default four redials, 100 ms doubling to 2 s; `RetryPolicy::none()` for one try): a failure of the transport waits, dials again (the correlation id and the credential ride the new connection's first frame), asks `Begin` where the transfer stands and sends from there. A refusal, an unexpected reply or a malformed frame is never retried. The Editor's publish path uses the default.
- **A reset or an end of stream is a transport error.** `AssetTransferClientError` gains `Protocol(String)`: a frame that will not parse, or is too large, is the peer's protocol failure; a reset, abort, broken pipe, end of stream, deadline or refused connect (`is_transport_failure`) is `Transport`, and `is_transport()` tells a caller which. The Editor's publish no longer maps a cut connection to `Protocol`: it reads as `Unreachable`, as its preview already did.

## Evidence (slices 2 to 4)
- `tests/resume.rs`, 17 tests, each cut a real connection killed by a proxy between a real client and a real listener: a transfer cut after about two and a half chunks resumes, every byte crosses once (the receiver's counter equals the asset's length) and the client does not resend held chunks; with no retry the cut is `Transport` and the chunks wait for the next connection; the retry gives up after its redials; the delays double to their cap and the wait is really taken; a duplicate chunk is acknowledged and counted once and a changed or shorter one refused; two uploads of one asset at once (four rounds) both succeed and the bytes are taken once; an asset already held is done on `Begin` and on a last chunk; a different length restarts; a failed verification leaves nothing; the 17th unfinished transfer drops the oldest; an end of stream is retried and reported `Transport`; a reply that is not the dialect is `Protocol` and not retried; a refusal is not retried; a receiver that resumes past the end is `Protocol`; and through the **engine's own receiver** (`AssetPublishServer` with a token) the credential rides the redial and a cut transfer resumes there too.
- `gx-asset-plane` 130 lib + 17 resume + 6 auth + 7 preview tests; `gx-editor` publish: 1 new (a connection that is dropped is `Unreachable`).
- Mutants, one at a time: 22; 21 failed a named test first time and one survived (the client ignoring `resume_from`: the receiver's idempotent duplicate hid it), so the test now also asserts the client's own count of bytes sent, and it is caught. None are equivalent.
- Gate: see the 19 November log.

## Not done
`AssetTransferServer` (the standalone transfer listener, used in tests only) is unauthenticated and loopback-only; the Editor needs `GX_ASSET_CREDENTIAL` set to use a protected engine (no field in the UI yet); the host's reading of an authentication refusal as "nothing listening" (finding 8) and the engine emitting `TransportFailed` for non-transport causes (the rest of finding 14) are not part of this row's acceptance and are still open.
